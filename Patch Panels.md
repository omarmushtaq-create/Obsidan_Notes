### Definition

A **patch panel** is a hardware assembly containing pre-wired ports on the front and termination blocks on the back. It serves as a central connection hub that connects in-wall office cabling to an Ethernet switch.

### Key Components & Connections

- **In-Wall Cabling:** Connects a computer's wall port to the back of the patch panel through cabling running through the walls.
    
- **IDC Punchdown Blocks:** Located on the **back** of the patch panel, where the in-wall cables are terminated using insulation displacement connectors (IDC).
    
- **RJ45 Ports:** Located on the **front** of the patch panel; these ports are pre-wired and ready for patch cords.
    
- **Patch Cords:** Short cables used to connect the front RJ45 ports of the patch panel directly into ports on an Ethernet switch.
    

### Primary Purpose & Benefit

- **Flexible Management:** Makes it easy to change or reconfigure how any specific wall port location connects to network switch ports without altering the permanent cabling inside the walls. It also prevents wear and tear on switch ports.
