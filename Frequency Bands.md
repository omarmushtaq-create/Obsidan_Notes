

## Key Concept
Every Wi-Fi device runs on a specific radio frequency, inside a bigger frequency **band**.

---

## Channels
- A **channel** = smaller slice of a frequency band.
- Acts like a "lane" so multiple networks can use the same band without interfering.
- Channel width = set by the frequency band + access point (AP) settings.
- All channels start the same width, BUT some standards let you **bond channels together** to make a wider one.
- **Wider channel = more data throughput.**

---

## The Three Main Frequency Bands

### 2.4 GHz
- Best at passing through walls/solid objects -> **longest range**
- Few channels available -> gets **congested** easily
- Shares space with Bluetooth, microwaves, etc. -> **more interference**
- **Lower max data rates**

### 5 GHz
- Worse at penetrating walls -> shorter range than 2.4 GHz
- More channels available -> **less congestion**
- **Higher data rates**, but shorter distance

### 6 GHz
- Newest band
- Worst at penetrating walls -> **shortest range**
- **Fastest speeds** of the three
- Less congestion (new/less used) -> more stable, reliable connection

---

## Range Comparison (Indoors, Nominal)

| Band    | Range (meters) | Range (feet) |
| ------- | -------------- | ------------ |
| 2.4 GHz | 45 m           | 150 ft       |
| 5 GHz   | 30 m           | 100 ft       |
| 6 GHz   | 15 m           | 50 ft        |

> Note: Full-speed connection is usually only reliable at **1/3 to 1/2** of these distances, depending on:
> - Wireless standard used
> - Obstacles/building materials
> - Interference from other radio sources

---

## Quick Summary (Trade-off Rule)
**Lower frequency = longer range, more interference, slower speed**
**Higher frequency = shorter range, less interference, faster speed**

---

## Other Notes
- Frequency bands are **regulated** (government/radio rules).
- There are **power output restrictions** -> another factor that limits range.
