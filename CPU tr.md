- Electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a sudden flow of electricity between two electrically charged objects caused by contact or a short circuit. ESD can damage sensitive electronic components, including CPUs, by creating a high-voltage surge that can destroy or degrade the delicate circuits within the processor. While CPUs are designed to last several years under normal operating conditions, they are highly sensitive to ESD during handling or installation. For example, if a technician does not use proper grounding techniques (such as wearing an anti-static wrist strap), static electricity from their body can discharge into the CPU, causing irreparable damage. This is why ESD is a common factor that might cause a CPU to fail prematurely.
