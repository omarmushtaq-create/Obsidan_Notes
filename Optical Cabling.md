Copper wires use electricity, which slows down over long distances and suffers from interference. Fiber optics use **light**, which has no electrical interference, loses less signal, runs for miles instead of feet, and carries much higher speeds (gigabits or terabits per second).

### How Fiber Cable is Built

1. **Core:** A tiny glass center that carries light.
    
2. **Cladding:** Glass or plastic around the core that keeps the light inside.
    
3. **Buffer:** A soft coating that protects the cladding.
    
4. **Jacket:** The outer plastic cover around the whole cable.
    

### The Two Types of Fiber

|Feature|Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)|Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)|
|---|---|---|
|**Glass Core**|Very thin (8–10 microns)|Thick (50 or 62.5 microns)|
|**Light Source**|Strong laser|Cheaper LED or VCSEL light|
|**Distance & Speed**|Very long distances (miles) & high speeds (10 Gbps+)|Shorter distances & lower speeds|
|**Best Used For**|Long connections across cities (WANs)|Local networks inside buildings (LANs)|
|**Cost**|More expensive|Cheaper to set up|

### Fiber Connectors

Connectors use a small plastic or ceramic tip (**ferrule**) to keep the light lined up.

- **ST (Straight Tip):** Push-and-twist style. Used mostly on older networks.
    
- **SC (Subscriber Connector):** Simple push-and-pull style. Used for both single-mode and multi-mode.
    
- **LC (Lucent Connector):** A mini push-and-pull connector. Smaller size so you can plug more cables into the same space.
    

**Important Handling Tips:**

- Fiber patch cables can have the same connector on both ends (like ST-to-ST) or different ones (like ST-to-SC).
    
- Connectors break easily—do not plug and unplug them constantly.
    
- Put **dust caps** over open ports and cable ends to keep dirt out.
### Patch Cords & Handling

- **Patch Cords:** Short fiber cables used to connect equipment. They can have matching connectors on both ends (like ST–ST) or mixed connectors (like ST–SC).
    
- **Care & Damage:** Fiber connectors break easily—do not plug and unplug them constantly.
    
- **Dust Caps:** Always cover unused connectors and ports with dust caps to keep dirt out.
